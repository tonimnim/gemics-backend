# Market research: paid-entry eFootball tournaments (October 2026)

Research, not legal advice. Every market needs local gaming and payments counsel
before Tonits charges an entry fee. Calling the fee an "administration fee" does
not protect you if a regulator decides it is really a stake.

## Summary

| Country | English / localization | Legal risk: paid entry + cash prize | Payment rails | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kenya | High (EF EPI 593, #19). English is enough | **Medium** | M-Pesa (STK Push in, B2C out) | Gambling Control Act 2025: broad "bet" definition; esports-as-sport is untested |
| Brazil | Low (466). **Portuguese required** | **Medium** | Pix (dLocal, EBANX) | Skill games sit outside the gambling contravention; prize tax; CPF KYC |
| Thailand | Very low (402). **Thai required** | **High** | PromptPay, TrueMoney (2C2P, Omise, Xendit) | Entry fees reportedly treated as wagers unless licensed |
| Indonesia | Low (471). **Bahasa required** | **High** | QRIS, DANA, OVO, GoPay (Xendit, Midtrans) | Art. 303 covers chance *or* skill played for stakes |
| Malaysia | High (581). English OK, Malay recommended | **High** | DuitNow, Touch 'n Go (Xendit, 2C2P) | Law covers "mixed chance and skill"; online gambling actively enforced |
| India | n/a | **Medium**, only if registered | UPI (Razorpay) | From May 2026, registered esports may charge cost-only fees |

**Bottom line:** Kenya and Brazil are workable with counsel's sign-off. Thailand,
Indonesia and Malaysia should launch with free entry and sponsored prizes. India
only through esports registration, with fees that just cover costs.

## Kenya
- **Law:** Gambling Control Act No. 14 of 2025 replaced the BCLB and the old Act and
  created the Gambling Regulatory Authority (GRA). The Licensing Regulations were
  gazetted on 30 June 2026.
  - "Game of chance" excludes "an athletic game or sport".
  - "Bet" means a stake on a game, sport or contingency.
  - "Pool betting" covers winnings that depend on the stakes paid.
  - A "prize competition" licence covers free entry only.
  - Players must be 18+.
- **Main risk:** GRA treats a paid entry as a bet. The risk is highest if prizes
  visibly come from entry fees. Fixed, organizer- or sponsor-funded prizes help.
  Get a written opinion or engage GRA directly. Watch the 20% withholding tax on
  winnings.
- Sources: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2025/14 ;
  https://cmadvocates.com/blog/kenya-gazettes-the-gambling-control-licensing-regulations-2026/
- **Market:** Esports Kenya Federation eFootball Mobile Cup (June 2025, KSh 15,000
  top prize). Competitors: IndexG Esports (KSh 5,000 pools), Fantasy44.

## Brazil
- Law 14.790/2023 regulates fixed-odds betting, including bets *on* e-sports. It
  does not regulate players competing for a prize themselves.
- Fantasy sport is exempt when the prize is guaranteed and independent of entrant
  numbers. That's a good template for Tonits.
- Skill-predominant games fall outside the "jogos de azar" contravention.
- Prize income is taxed (30% withheld at source, or the progressive table).
- Pix for collection and payout through dLocal or EBANX, with CPF-matched KYC.
- #1 country for lifetime eFootball downloads.

## Thailand
- Gambling Act B.E. 2478. Skill esports are generally outside it, but an entry fee
  may be treated as a wager unless licensed (unconfirmed, but serious).
- Foreign organizers may need a Foreign Business License.
- Use free entry with sponsored prizes.
- #2 worldwide for eFootball revenue.

## Indonesia
- Criminal Code art. 303 is commonly read to cover chance *or* skill played for
  stakes. Komdigi blocks platforms aggressively.
- Partner with PB ESI and use free entry with sponsored prizes.
- There is an informal WhatsApp tournament scene with entry-fee pools. Real
  demand, but legally exposed.
- #2 for downloads; official PSSI–Konami partnership.

## Malaysia
- Common Gaming Houses Act covers "mixed chance and skill" played for money. The
  Court of Appeal applied it to online gambling in 2023. MCMC removed about 458k
  gambling items between January 2025 and May 2026.
- Use free entry with sponsored prizes.

## India
- The Online Gaming Act 2025 bans money games, skill or chance.
- Registered e-sports may charge cost-only participation fees and pay
  performance prizes.

## Competitors
The space is fragmented, not empty. In Kenya: IndexG Esports, EKF series,
Fantasy44. In Indonesia: informal WhatsApp tournaments. Globally: Challengermode,
Skillz, Repeat.gg. Konami's own events are free to enter. No licensed,
eFootball-specific paid-entry app with local payments was found in these markets.

## Ways to reduce risk everywhere
1. Fund prizes from the organizer or sponsors, fixed in advance and independent of
   entries. Never show a "pool". Tonits already models entry fees as
   administration fees, never a prize pool.
2. Offer a free-entry route to the same prizes.
3. 18+ only, with KYC, and pay out only to the verified player's own account.
4. Withhold tax on prizes where required.
5. No chance mechanics.
6. Get recognition from the national esports federation (EKF, PB ESI) and engage
   the regulator directly.
7. Get local counsel's opinion before enabling paid entry.

## Product signal: team play
eFootball supports co-op 2v2 and 3v3 through manual friend rooms and occasional
events, one player per mobile device. As of September 2026 it has **no permanent
team matchmaking** (https://www.chocobonplan.com/news-jeux-video/efootball-2027-le-gameplay-progresse-mais-le-2v2-se-fait-attendre).
Organized team tournaments are an open gap Tonits can fill.
